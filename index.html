How to read a supplement label

  • Amounts, not just names. A list of ingredients without amounts tells you little.
  • Proprietary blends. If the amount of each ingredient is hidden, you cannot compare it with anything.
  • Interactions. Ask a doctor or pharmacist if you take medicines, are pregnant or are breastfeeding.
  • Third-party testing. Look for an independent test report, not just a logo.

Red flags in supplement marketing

  • Claims that it fixes or removes nerve pain.
  • Customer reviews on the seller's own page that you cannot verify.
  • A countdown timer or "last bottles" pressure.
  • A site that calls itself the "official" source but only forwards you to a checkout.
